>[!d] Definition
> Every mathematical result has a **hypothesis**, the conditions assumed, and a **conclusion**, what is guaranteed whenever they hold. In "if $H$, then $C$", "if" introduces the hypothesis $H$ and "then" the conclusion $C$. The result asserts nothing where $H$ fails.

>[!e] Worked example
> "If $a$ and $b$ are even integers, then $ab$ is even": hypothesis "$a$ and $b$ are even integers", conclusion "$ab$ is even". Without "if … then" the hypothesis may be implicit: "the square of an odd integer is odd" has hypothesis "$n$ is an odd integer", conclusion "$n^2$ is odd"; "the angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$" assumes Euclidean geometry.

>[!warning] Common error
> Invoking a result whose hypothesis fails. The first result above says nothing about $2 \cdot 3$ or $3 \cdot 5$, whatever their parity.
