>[!d] Definition
> For $g\in G$, the **cyclic subgroup generated by** $g$ is $\langle g\rangle=\{g^k:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ (additively, $\{kg:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$). $G$ is **cyclic**, with **generator** $g$, if $G=\langle g\rangle$. Then $\langle g\rangle$ is the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $g$, $|\langle g\rangle|=|g|$, and every cyclic group is abelian.

>[!p] Proof
> $e=g^0\in\langle g\rangle$ and $g^k(g^l)^{-1}=g^{k-l}$, so $\langle g\rangle\le G$ by the subgroup test; a subgroup containing $g$ contains every $g^k$, being closed under products and inverses. If $|g|=n$, Order divides exponent gives $g^k=g^r$ for $r$ the remainder of $k$ modulo $n$, with $e,g,\dots,g^{n-1}$ distinct, so $|\langle g\rangle|=n$; if $|g|=\infty$, the $g^k$ are distinct. Finally $g^ag^b=g^{a+b}=g^bg^a$. $\square$

>[!e] Examples
> $\mathbb{Z}=\langle1\rangle=\langle-1\rangle$, and $\langle n\rangle=n\mathbb{Z}$. $\mathbb{Z}_{10}^*=\{[1],[3],[7],[9]\}=\langle[3]\rangle$, the powers of $[3]$ being $[3],[9],[7],[1]$. $\mathbb{Z}_8^*=\{[1],[3],[5],[7]\}$ is abelian but not cyclic: $[3]^2=[5]^2=[7]^2=[1]$, so each $\langle[a]\rangle$ has at most $2$ elements.
