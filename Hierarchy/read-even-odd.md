>[!d] Definition
> An integer $n$ is **even** if there exists $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $n = 2m$, and **odd** if there exists $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $n = 2m + 1$.

>[!info] Note (equivalent forms)
> $n$ is even if and only if $2 \mid n$. That every integer is **exactly one** of even and odd is not part of the definition but a theorem: the Division Algorithm with divisor $2$, whose unique remainder is $0$ or $1$. It licenses replacing "not even" by "odd" in proofs.

>[!e] Worked example
> $0 = 2 \cdot 0$ and $-4 = 2 \cdot (-2)$ are even; $-7 = 2 \cdot (-4) + 1$ is odd. The witness must be an integer: $7 = 2 \cdot 3.5$ does not make $7$ even, as $3.5 \notin \mathbb{Z}$; $7 = 2 \cdot 3 + 1$ is odd.
