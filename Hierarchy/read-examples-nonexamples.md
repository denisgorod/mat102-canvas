>[!s] Method
> After reading a definition, test it on a varied sample: several objects that satisfy it, at least one that does not, and edge cases ($0$, negatives, extremes). Decide each case by the condition alone, stating why it qualifies or not.

>[!e] Worked example
> Recall $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ is even if $n = 2m$ for some $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, and call $n$ **quirky** if $n^2$ is not even. Then $1$, $-3$ and $5$ are quirky: their squares $1, 9, 25$ equal $2m$ only for $m = \tfrac12, \tfrac92, \tfrac{25}{2} \notin \mathbb{Z}$. But $2$ is not quirky, as $2^2 = 4 = 2 \cdot 2$; nor is the edge case $0$, as $0^2 = 0 = 2 \cdot 0$.

>[!info] Note
> Each verdict comes from the condition, not from resemblance to earlier cases; intuition built this way is grounded in the definition.
