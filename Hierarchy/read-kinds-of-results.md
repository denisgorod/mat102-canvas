>[!d] Definition
> A **mathematical result** is a precise claim about previously defined objects, labelled by its role:
> - **Lemma:** a stepping stone, used to prove something larger.
> - **Proposition:** a useful result of moderate importance.
> - **Theorem:** a major or central result.
> - **Corollary:** a result that follows almost immediately from a theorem.

>[!info] Note
> The labels signal importance, not degree of truth: each is a proven claim. Historical names can mislead: Euclid's Lemma is central to unique factorisation. In logic, "proposition" also means any statement that is true or false.

>[!e] Worked example
> **Proposition.** If $a$ and $b$ are even integers, then $ab$ is even.
> By the definition of even, $a = 2m$ and $b = 2n$ with $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $ab = 2(2mn)$ with $2mn \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $ab$ is even.
