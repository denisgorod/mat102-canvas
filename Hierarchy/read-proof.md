>[!d] Definition
> A **proof** of a result is a finite sequence of statements, from the hypothesis to the conclusion, each justified by the hypothesis, a definition, an axiom, a previously proven result, or logical deduction from earlier statements. An assertion records belief; a proof shows why the conclusion must hold.

>[!info] Note (certainty)
> A valid proof establishes the result in every case satisfying the hypothesis, without exception, and no later evidence revises it. Errors live in flawed proofs, not in proven results; the certainty is relative to the accepted axioms.

>[!e] Worked example
> If $n$ is an even integer, then $n^2$ is even.
> - $n = 2k$ with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$: hypothesis, definition of even.
> - $n^2 = 2(2k^2)$: algebra.
> - $2k^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$: products of integers are integers.
> - $n^2$ is even: definition of even. $\square$
