---
title: "A syllabus is a list; learning is a path"
role: beat
---

A syllabus is a **storage format**. It's what you get when you flatten a web of ideas into
something that fits on one page and survives a committee.

Flattening is lossy, and what it loses is exactly the part students need: the *reason* one idea
follows another.

| The syllabus records | The student needs |
|---|---|
| Week 4: Congruences | Why would anyone invent congruence? |
| Week 5: $\mathbb{Z}_n$ | What problem did the last idea leave open? |

>[!idea]
The list isn't wrong. It's just a projection — and we've been teaching from the shadow.
